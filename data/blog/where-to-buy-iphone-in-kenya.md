Kenya has plenty of places to buy an iPhone, and each has trade-offs. This guide compares them in general terms, because prices on marketplaces change daily and differ from seller to seller.

## Physical shops in Nairobi

Buying from a shop lets you hold the phone, test it and walk back in if something goes wrong. The downside is a trip into town, so message first to confirm stock.

## Jumia and other online marketplaces

Jumia and Kilimall list iPhones from many sellers, including refurbished phones. They are convenient for comparing prices, and many buyers search them first.

- **Good for:** quick price checks and home delivery.
- **Watch for:** condition wording, whether the warranty comes from the seller or the marketplace, and the return window.

## Jiji and other classifieds

Jiji usually has the lowest asking prices because many sellers are individuals. It also carries the most risk. Never send a deposit, meet in a safe public place and follow our [used iPhone checklist](post:refurbished-iphones-in-kenya).

## Safaricom shops

Safaricom sells selected iPhones and offers Lipa Mdogo Mdogo on some phones. Its range and pricing change, so check with Safaricom directly. Read our [instalments guide](post:buy-iphone-on-installments-in-kenya) before signing up, because the total cost can be well above the cash price.

## How to compare two prices fairly

Ask every seller the same five questions:

- Is it Brand New, Ex-UK or refurbished?
- How much storage does it have?
- Is it eSIM only, or physical SIM + eSIM?
- What is the battery health?
- What warranty is included, and who honours it?
